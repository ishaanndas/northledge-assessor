# Quill Robotics — Seed Round (May 2026)

## Slide 1: Apparel is the last unautomated manufacturing industry
Garments are still sewn by hand. 60 million people work in apparel manufacturing and labor is 25 to 35% of garment cost. Brands want to reshore production but cannot find the sewing labor to do it.

## Slide 2: Quill sews
Quill builds robotic sewing cells. A vision system tracks fabric deformation in real time and a two-arm manipulator feeds the garment through a standard industrial sewing head. One cell replaces 2.5 sewing operators on simple seams: side seams, hems, pocket attachment.

## Slide 3: Traction
$1.4M ARR. Three paying customers: two US contract manufacturers and one brand-owned facility in Mexico. 14 cells deployed. Pipeline of $6.2M with signed LOIs.

## Slide 4: Market
Warehouse and industrial automation is a $48B market growing 17% a year (McKinsey, 2024). Sewing automation is the largest untouched segment within it.

## Slide 5: Business model
Robotics-as-a-service. $4,200 per cell per month on 36-month terms, including maintenance. Hardware cost per cell is $31,000 at current volume; payback on hardware is 8 months.

## Slide 6: Team
Elena Marsh (CEO) led perception at Tesla and spent four years in apparel sourcing at Gap Inc. Raj Venkat (CTO) did his PhD on deformable object manipulation at CMU and co-authored the DeformNet benchmark. Team of nine.

## Slide 7: Raise
$4M seed to scale to 60 cells, hire a field service team and open a Mexico service hub.
