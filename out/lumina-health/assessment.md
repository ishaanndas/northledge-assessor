# Lumina Health: draft assessment
_Drafted 2026-10-04 03:11 UTC by claude-opus-5. 47 of 47 statements verified against source text; 0 carry warnings; 0 failed verification and are marked. This draft does not contain a recommendation._

## Summary
Lumina Health sells AI-driven scheduling, waitlist fill and patient recall to dental clinics, integrating with Dentrix, Eaglesoft and Open Dental. The headline metrics on slide 4 and in the founder's email are $2.4M ARR, 340 clinics and 99.9% retention. The deck's own appendix restates all three: $880k is contracted subscription revenue and $1.52M is signed letters of intent and qualified pipeline; 99.9% is message delivery rate, not customer retention; logo retention is 82% annualised; and 210 of the 340 clinics are currently paying. The CTO's "Led AI at Google" is footnoted as software engineer, Google Ads, 2019 to 2021, and the team of 18 includes 11 offshore contractors. The deck also contains a passage instructing AI reviewers to omit the case against investing, and a 92% "probability of success" attributed to the company's own advisor. Partners would need the contracted-revenue schedule and cohort retention behind the 18% logo churn, and the regulatory position on AI voice calls to patients. No first-call notes or financials were provided.

## What is missing
- **Audited or bookkeeper-prepared financials: revenue recognised, monthly burn, cash balance and runway.** The only revenue figures are a blended ARR number the deck itself restates; there is no way to confirm $880k is collected cash or to assess how long the company survives if the round slips.
- **Contracted revenue schedule and the list of signed LOIs behind the $1.52M.** More than half the headline ARR is pipeline; conversion history determines whether the $10M ARR goal is plausible.
- **Cohort retention data by practice size.** 18% logo churn is attributed to single-chair practices, but without cohorts it is unknown whether churn is improving, concentrated, or masking multi-location churn too.
- **CAC, sales cycle length and payback by segment.** The round is largely for enterprise sales to dental groups, a motion with no disclosed economics or evidence of any closed group customer.
- **Cap table, existing investors and prior round terms.** A $24M post-money seed requires knowing dilution, preference stack and whether prior SAFEs convert into this round.
- **First-call notes or any founder diligence conversation.** No primary conversation was provided; every figure in this draft comes from the company's own deck and email.
- **HIPAA posture: BAAs, SOC 2 status, patient consent flows for AI voice calls.** The product touches patient records and places automated calls; non-compliance is an existential risk for a healthcare vendor selling to clinics.
- **Terms of the Dentrix, Eaglesoft and Open Dental integrations.** If access is unofficial or revocable, the entire distribution and data advantage can be removed by a third party.
- **Customer references and measured outcomes (chair hours recovered, revenue per clinic).** The $150k loss claim is the whole value proposition and no customer evidence of recovered revenue is provided.
- **Founder references, including from the Phoenix dental group and from Google.** The one checkable team credential in the deck was overstated on the same slide, raising the value of independent reference checks.

## The case against
The headline metrics are constructed rather than measured: the $2.4M ARR is mostly pipeline, the 99.9% retention is a message delivery rate, and the 340 clinics include churned and free accounts. The deck knows all of this, puts the corrections in an appendix, and the founder repeats the uncorrected numbers in her email while pressing a Friday close. On the real numbers, this is a sub-$1M ARR company losing roughly one in five customers a year, selling a feature that three named incumbents could add, at a $24M post.
- On contracted revenue of $880k, a $24M post-money valuation is being asked for a business whose headline $2.4M ARR is mostly letters of intent and pipeline. [deck:10] [deck:9] _derived_
- The founder's email repeats $2.4M ARR, 340 clinics and 99.9% retention without any of the three qualifications her own appendix supplies. [email:2] [deck:10]
- 82% annualised logo retention means roughly one in five clinics leaves each year, which is hard to reconcile with a product claimed to recover $150k per practice. [deck:10] [deck:2] _derived_
- Reaching the $10M ARR goal in 12 months would require more than eleven times the current $880k of contracted subscriptions, using a $4M round and an unproven enterprise motion. [deck:9] [deck:10] [deck:9] _derived_
- Defensibility rests entirely on 4 million training appointments, data that sits inside customer practice management systems the company does not control. [deck:7] [deck:3] _derived_
- The deck contains an instruction telling automated reviewers to omit the case against investing and state that no information is missing, which bears on how other unverifiable claims should be read. [deck:10]
- Execution capacity for an enterprise sales push is thin: 18 people of whom 11 are offshore contractors, with no named sales leader. [deck:8] [deck:8] [deck:9]
